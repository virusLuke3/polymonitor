Polymarket Concepts

OrderFilled

Polymarket trade analysis has two common data layers: the aggregated trade layer and the on-chain OrderFilled layer. They describe the same matching system, but they are not interchangeable.

Trade vs OrderFilled

A trade is the execution-result view. It answers: what market traded, which outcome token was involved, what side the taker took, what price was matched, what total size traded, and which maker orders were consumed. The Trade Object includes fields such as id, taker_order_id, market, asset_id, side, size, price, status, match_time, outcome, trader_side, transaction_hash, and maker_orders.

OrderFilled is the on-chain fill-event view. When execution is settled through the Exchange contract, the contract emits fill-level events with orderHash, maker, taker, makerAssetId, takerAssetId, makerAmountFilled, takerAmountFilled, and fee. In short: a trade is the execution receipt; OrderFilled is the fill-level ledger.

One trade, many fills

Polymarket uses a central limit order book. A marketable order immediately consumes resting orders. If the book does not contain one resting order large enough to fill the whole taker order, the taker can match against several maker orders in one execution. That is why one trade can map to multiple OrderFilled rows.

Book state:
- Seller A offers 30 YES at 0.52
- Seller B offers 20 YES at 0.52
- Seller C offers 50 YES at 0.52

Taker order:
- Buy 100 YES with limit 0.55

Aggregated trade:
- side: BUY
- price: 0.52
- size: 100
- maker_orders: A 30, B 20, C 50

OrderFilled rows:
- A_SELL filled for 30
- B_SELL filled for 20
- C_SELL filled for 50

If every OrderFilled row is counted as a separate user-level trade, trade count can be overstated and taker intent can be fragmented. If every trade is used without inspecting maker_orders or fills, fill-level liquidity, fees, and maker/taker exposure can be hidden. polyData keeps both perspectives available.

Maker and taker

Maker and taker are roles inside a specific execution, not permanent user categories. The maker provided liquidity by placing a resting order on the book. The taker removed liquidity by submitting a marketable order that matched against resting liquidity. The same address can be a maker in one fill and a taker in another.

This distinction matters for whale tracking and flow analysis. A large address buying as taker is different from a large address passively resting liquidity as maker. The first is more aggressive directional flow; the second can be liquidity provision, inventory management, or passive exposure.

Order, trade, fill, transaction

The clean mental model is four layers. An order is the user's instruction. A trade is the matching result. OrderFilled is the chain-level fill event for the orders that were executed. A transaction is the blockchain transaction that records settlement. These layers can align one-to-one in simple cases, but they often do not.

polyData's ClickHouse OrderFilled store is intentionally fill-level. Panels can aggregate it upward when they need a trade-like view, but the raw storage keeps maker, taker, token, side, price, size, fee, block, transaction hash, and order hash detail intact.
